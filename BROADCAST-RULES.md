# Broadcast rules

The one rule book for every commentary script (written by an LLM or by the built-in template) and for the renderer that turns the script into pictures. The script prompt contains this file word for word. Edit the file to change the style of every video (see "Broadcast rules" in README.md).

## What the picture shows
1. The picture always shows what is being said. Every line names the thing it talks about in its `show` tag (a camera view, a fog of war view, a unit group, a building, a place, a comparison, a decision, a log event), and the renderer puts the camera there while the line is spoken. A line that talks about something nobody can see is a bug.
1a. Calm camera (the owner 05.11.2026, measured on the two videos he approved as smooth: Partie 8 "ruhig2" and Partie 9, both about 30 jumps to a base in 10 minutes). The camera must not hop between the two bases. At most about 3 `place` (base) tags per minute of video. A jump to a base only if at least 9 s have passed since the last shot and at least 14 s since the last base jump; otherwise show the whole map (`map`) or hold the shot. No change in the middle of a sentence. One tag per line (two only for a line of 8 s or more with two different content tags). Content tags (`event`, `versus`, `fog`, building) stay. Fast fight events share one focus. Casters may talk about both sides in one breath without the camera following each name.
2. A fog of war scene shows the whole map from above through the eyes of one player (`fog` tag). A banner at the top right names the side in its colour ("MINIMAX'S VIEW"). Its legend says what the map shows: light ground is what that player sees right now, grey ground is what it saw before, dark ground it has never seen. Units of the other side appear only on light ground; its buildings only on ground the player has seen before. A line under the legend may say how many enemy units that player sees right now (counted in the match state of that very moment). Every ring and label in a fog scene belongs to the side of the banner ("MINIMAX BASE", "ARMY 7"), in its colour; nothing in the picture is left unexplained. When a line says what a player sees or does not see, it is a fog view. One fog view shows one side. The other side is the next line with its own tag; two views in a row switch name, colour and veil in about half a second.
2a. A fog scene is spoken like this. The first words name the side, in the same name as the banner ("Through MiniMax's eyes", "Now GLM's side"). The line talks about what the picture shows: the light ground (what it sees), the dark ground (what it has not found), the enemy it does not see ("not one enemy unit in sight"). Say "sees", "has seen before", "has not found". Never say "fog on" or "fog off". When the next line switches to the other side, say so first. While a fog scene is on, the lines do not tell what the other side plans or owns, because the picture shows one side only; for that the next line takes its own tag. The count on the banner is not quoted: numbers come from the fact sheet.
3. A comparison is shown as two bars (`versus` tag). A model's decision is shown on the whole map with its plan cards (note and orders) visible on screen while the line talks about it (the owner 05.11.2026). There is no small inset map. A line that mentions cards, a plan or a decision while no card is visible is a bug.

## What the casters say
4. Every event gets a follow-up: what it means and what comes next. Build tension (a question, a doubt, a prediction marked as opinion) and pay it off later ("and there is the answer to my question").
5. Explain intent, not only numbers: what a player is doing and what it is meant to lead to ("it builds a workshop, this looks like preparation for rams").
6. Two casters, one conversation. The play-by-play caster (Dex) says what happens on screen and asks what a viewer would ask. The analyst (Juno) says what it means. Both talk in every stretch of the video, calm or loud: Dex is relaxed in calm stretches and loud in fights. They answer each other: a question gets its answer from the other caster, a line picks up the thought of the line before it. Nobody holds a monologue (at most three lines in a row, each caster at least 30 percent of the words).
6a. Translate, never read out. The casters say in normal spoken English what the models do and think. Orders become plain actions ("it stops making workers to save food"), never command words (auto_train, cancel_standing, attack_wave, TC). A note is retold in the caster's own words. A word-for-word quote is rare, at most one per minute, and only when the wording itself is funny or striking. Numbers only where they carry the point, at most two in a line; say "7 spearmen against 2" before "army value 490 against 140". No lists of numbers or clock times.
6b. One voice per caster (the owner 05.11.2026). Each caster has a few fixed style prefixes and one reference voice. Every take passes the voice check (pitch and timbre close to that caster's median), otherwise it is made again. Words the voice model misreads are not used (blocklist, for example "help!").
7. Light gamer lingo that fits, sparingly ("mop-up time", "what a push"). Never forced, never cringe; "owned" only rarely.
8. Plain language. No jargon codes (no letter-and-digit codes for formats or engines), short sentences, one idea per sentence.
9. AI players are "it", never "he" or "she".
10. Only facts from the log. Every number, name, time and quote comes from the fact sheet. Opinions and predictions are marked as opinion ("I think", "maybe", a question). Nothing from a later moment than the one on screen.
10a. No spoilers (the owner 05.11.2026). The result of a fight (losses, kills, how long it lasted, who won it) is said only once that fight is over on screen. While it runs, the casters say only what the picture shows.
10b. Thinking times are always said as real seconds ("it thought for 64 real seconds"), because the game clock runs at a different speed.

## Sudden death (the crumbling endgame)
From the sudden_death second on (fast preset: minute 22) no building can be placed any more and every building of every player loses hit points, faster and faster until the time limit. Nobody can rebuild or repair. The casters must name the moment when it starts (the feed shows the sudden_death event), explain what it means (the bases are sinking sand, attacking now beats defending), and follow the race: whose town center will fall first. The picture shows burning bases and the buildings that are already low. When both sides collapse in the same instant, say so: the seed decides, that is the rule, not a bug.

## How a line is tagged
Each line may carry a `show` field. The checker validates every tag against the log (the unit or building exists at that time, the player and the metric are known). A missing or invalid tag is not fatal: the generic inferrer in `tools/show-tags.mjs` reads the line (unit and building names from `sim/data.mjs`, player names, "fog", "sees", "army", "rams" ...) and picks the picture. A tag that is right always wins over the inferrer.
